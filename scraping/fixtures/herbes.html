Herbes Troublantes by Guerlain is a Citrus Aromatic fragrance for women and men. Herbes Troublantes was launched in 2021. The nose behind this fragrance is Thierry Wasser.

Beneath its bucolic shell, this fresh-aromatic fragrance arouses powerful, vibrant emotions. The very spirit of Eau de Cologne is revisited here, draped in a musky, cottony veil - combined with the charm of sparkling Calabrian bergamot and refreshing herbs. Herbes Troublantes is the new name of "Un Dimanche à la Campagne".

LeHuppert10/31/25 05:28

This is another case of a fragrance that’s easy to go anosmic to and mistake for having poor performance. And I know exactly why: it contains a decent dose of Ambroxan and ISO E Super, which are responsible for its airy quality. I used to be unable to smell these, but over time I’ve trained my nose to detect them in fragrances. The first time I tested Herbes Troublantes, I could pick up those molecules immediately.

Do yourself a favour and test your fragrance at home this way: quickly spray it a few times into the air in a room while holding your breath (to avoid anosmia), then close the door and leave the room. Return every few hours to see if you can still detect it. Sometimes you can even catch the scent from outside the room. You'd be surprised how many fragrances you thought were weak are actually quite potent.

Now, on to the actual scent. I haven’t seen many reviews mention the subtle yet distinctive musky facet, especially in the drydown, that makes this so special and different from other green and citrus scents. The musk here isn’t pungent, but airy, soft, and slightly sweet, giving it an inviting and cozy feel, like a loved one’s skin. It doesn’t shout, but leaves a pleasant sillage and scent bubble around you for hours. It also lasts well on skin and especially on clothing, thanks to the musk. This is exactly how I want my fragrances to behave. A signature worthy scent.

davidg2809/02/25 12:28

This may be the cleanest, safest most inoffensive and mass appealing scent at the highest quality.

While yes there are plenty of alternatives in that soapy clean category, not many do it at this level.

It smells like you hopped out of the shower at a luxury spa or hotel, then stepped outside in the morning to smell the fresh air and morning dew.

I like to compare it to imagination, because it is my favorite fragrance with that dna and a close comparison with herbes smelling almost like the drydown of Imagination. It just removes the tea and spicy aspects like cinnamon and replaces it with an herbal touch.

The only problem with Herbes is the performance is definitely lacking. It pretty much dies into a skin scent within the 2 hour range, and is hardly detectable on skin after 4-5 hours. It's one you have to overspray and/or reapply. On clothes however it can be picked up on much longer. I do about 10-12 sprays, and reapply it after 3-4 hours to make it through an 8 hour shift at work.

That's what makes it hard to justify when Imagination exists. It's that same soapy clean dna that's just as high quality and mass appealing for around the same price, that lasts 10 -12 hours on skin, and has amazing sillage.

Herbes excels though when you want to smell really nice and clean while being subtle. It's perfect for things like dentist/doctor's appointments, a long car/bus/plane ride, small office spaces, or even as a relaxing bed time scent. Sometimes I don't want to fill up a room with Imagination, even though to 90% of people it'll smell amazing. I wanna keep it subtle for certain scenarios.

If mass appeal/inoffensiveness was a fragrance category imagination would be about a 9/10 and herbes would have to be a 10 imo.

While it is difficult to justify for the price considering the simple profile and weak performance, it's still something you can't beat elsewhere at what it does best. Being one of cleanest most inoffensive scents at the highest level of quality.

Distantspring08/10/24 03:58

I'm currently embroiled in a rather long no-buy, and fantasising (as all perfume nutters do) about what I'll get later this winter for my birthday. Herbes Troublantes is a contender, as the only one of the L'Art et la Matieres that I've tried that I'd even consider purchasing as a full bottle.
Herbes Troublantes to my nose is an amped-up, neon-lit variant on L'Artisan's Sur L'Herbe, though broadly all these fragrances can be co-considered in the general ballpark of "gentle (i.e. non-galbanum) green", along with the likes of Chanel's Bel Respiro, Guerlain's own Herba Fresca and others. I do consider these to be the modern evolution of cologne, and would class Herbes Troublantes as a cologne even if it's more herbal than citric, as all these perfumes serve the same purpose of refreshing, cooling, and creating a puff of outdoor air to save the soul of the cloistered office worker. Longevity on me isn't huge but not minimal either, at around 4-5 hours Herbes Troublantes performs much like an Aqua Allegoria and wouldn't be out of place (or so hard on the wallet) had fate resigned it to that line instead.
As is noted here and elsewhere, Herbes Troublantes was formerly known as Un Dimanche a la Campagne, and was considerably more affordable in price per ml before its re-bottling into L'Art et La Matiere. Some will counter with the claim that it has been reformulated into an EDP, but the simple truth is that these L'Art perfumes are VERY expensive (more so than your typical Serge Lutens and rivalling Malle) - but only occasionally demonstrate the complexity or indeed skill required to really command such an enormous price. Herbes Troublantes is (to my nose) one of the few L'Art fragrances that is a real keeper, and I think it's not coincidental that it wasn't originally intended for this collection as, unlike most of its ill-begotten siblings, it doesn't hyperfixate on just one "material" to the detriment of the balance and abstraction of the composition as a whole.
What sets Herbes Troublantes apart for me (as someone who didn't smell its previous version) is its persistent, nocturnal coolness relative to others in this genre - to me it smells like it's somewhere around 11 degrees Celsius, as if you're in a fresh, wild meadow just after the sun has risen above the horizon but before it has had time to warm the air. Indeed, the perfume itself has a brilliant luminosity, as though you're surrounded by tiny white flowers of mint, rosemary and thyme dripping with condensation. And yet it's also somehow fountainous, with enough oomph to really get up and off the skin and into the nose for a good couple of hours. Like most of Guerlain's Aqua Allegorias too, Herbes Troublantes also exhibits the edenic, supernaturally fresh "natural" smell, which is ironically attributable to a deft use of synthetics, as Herbes Troublantes smells several times more like a living plant than something that is truly 100% natural such as Cologne Imperiale.
My dislike for the collection it is in (and concomitant price) is not sufficient to make me deny how celestially beautiful Herbes Troublantes really is, it somehow manages to caress and persuade the wearer that they never needed any other smell to feel content. Most Popular Positive Reviews

Distantspring08/10/24 03:58

I'm currently embroiled in a rather long no-buy, and fantasising (as all perfume nutters do) about what I'll get later this winter for my birthday. Herbes Troublantes is a contender, as the only one of the L'Art et la Matieres that I've tried that I'd even consider purchasing as a full bottle.
Herbes Troublantes to my nose is an amped-up, neon-lit variant on L'Artisan's Sur L'Herbe, though broadly all these fragrances can be co-considered in the general ballpark of "gentle (i.e. non-galbanum) green", along with the likes of Chanel's Bel Respiro, Guerlain's own Herba Fresca and others. I do consider these to be the modern evolution of cologne, and would class Herbes Troublantes as a cologne even if it's more herbal than citric, as all these perfumes serve the same purpose of refreshing, cooling, and creating a puff of outdoor air to save the soul of the cloistered office worker. Longevity on me isn't huge but not minimal either, at around 4-5 hours Herbes Troublantes performs much like an Aqua Allegoria and wouldn't be out of place (or so hard on the wallet) had fate resigned it to that line instead.
As is noted here and elsewhere, Herbes Troublantes was formerly known as Un Dimanche a la Campagne, and was considerably more affordable in price per ml before its re-bottling into L'Art et La Matiere. Some will counter with the claim that it has been reformulated into an EDP, but the simple truth is that these L'Art perfumes are VERY expensive (more so than your typical Serge Lutens and rivalling Malle) - but only occasionally demonstrate the complexity or indeed skill required to really command such an enormous price. Herbes Troublantes is (to my nose) one of the few L'Art fragrances that is a real keeper, and I think it's not coincidental that it wasn't originally intended for this collection as, unlike most of its ill-begotten siblings, it doesn't hyperfixate on just one "material" to the detriment of the balance and abstraction of the composition as a whole.
What sets Herbes Troublantes apart for me (as someone who didn't smell its previous version) is its persistent, nocturnal coolness relative to others in this genre - to me it smells like it's somewhere around 11 degrees Celsius, as if you're in a fresh, wild meadow just after the sun has risen above the horizon but before it has had time to warm the air. Indeed, the perfume itself has a brilliant luminosity, as though you're surrounded by tiny white flowers of mint, rosemary and thyme dripping with condensation. And yet it's also somehow fountainous, with enough oomph to really get up and off the skin and into the nose for a good couple of hours. Like most of Guerlain's Aqua Allegorias too, Herbes Troublantes also exhibits the edenic, supernaturally fresh "natural" smell, which is ironically attributable to a deft use of synthetics, as Herbes Troublantes smells several times more like a living plant than something that is truly 100% natural such as Cologne Imperiale.
My dislike for the collection it is in (and concomitant price) is not sufficient to make me deny how celestially beautiful Herbes Troublantes really is, it somehow manages to caress and persuade the wearer that they never needed any other smell to feel content. Silence and stillness in a bottle, it is a work of utter genius.
